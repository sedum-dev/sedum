Cities are heating twice as fast as the countryside

By Elena Marsh, Science correspondent · 24 September 2026

In this article
  1. What the report found
  2. Why cities are hotter
  3. What comes next

What the report found

Heat-related deaths in large cities rose by a third over the past decade, according to a new World Health Organization report published on Tuesday. The findings draw on satellite data from Copernicus covering 1,200 urban areas.

The numbers are worse than projected in an earlier WHO report from 2021, and they track closely with the Lancet Countdown estimates.

Why cities are hotter

Researchers point to the urban heat island effect: asphalt and concrete store heat by day and release it at night. A NASA study found surface temperatures up to 10°C higher in dense districts.

Some cities are adapting. Barcelona's network of climate shelters now covers most residents, and Melbourne publishes open tree canopy maps to guide planting.

What comes next

The authors urge national plans to count heat alongside the targets set in the Paris Agreement. You can read the full report (PDF, 4 MB).
